Week 3 Scenario Quiz

Learning objectives: Distinguish a conversational response from a goal-directed workflow that can use tools and take actions.; Explain how instructions, current context, memory, tools, and orchestration shape an agent’s behavior.; Design a useful multi-step workflow with relevant information, narrow capabilities, checks, and stop conditions.; Match permissions and human review to an action’s impact, reversibility, uncertainty, privacy, and affected people.; Evaluate an agent’s result against evidence and the original goal, then choose to accept, revise, retry, or stop.

Week 3: Autonomy and Oversight

Scenario-based formative assessment. Choose the response that best preserves the learning goal, checks evidence, and keeps consequential decisions with an appropriate person.

Question 1. A family helper has reliably drafted weekly meal plans. The parent wants it to add ingredients to a shopping cart. What is a sensible next step?
Adding items changes a service but does not need to become a purchase; review the cart and ingredients before considering any payment authority.
Question 2. An agent drafts a message to all parents in a sports team about a schedule change. What should happen before it is sent?
A group message affects many people and may be difficult to retract. Review the wording, recipients, and source of the schedule change first.
Question 3. A parent asks an agent to decide whether a child’s new symptom needs urgent care. What is the safest role for the agent?
A generated response cannot examine the child or replace qualified medical advice. The caregiver should contact appropriate professional or emergency services when needed.
Question 4. A system can edit a shared family calendar. Which factor most strongly argues for a review checkpoint before it makes a change?
Shared changes affect other people and may create confusion even when technically reversible, so review can prevent harmful effects.
Question 5. A family is considering an agent for organizing a teenager’s activities. Which first pilot is best?
A narrow, low-stakes draft using limited information lets the family evaluate usefulness while preserving privacy and control.
Question 6. A family wants a chatbot to suggest public library events for a child. Which data practice is best?
Use the least information needed for the search, such as a general area and age range, and avoid exposing identity or account data.
Question 7. An approval screen says “The agent wants to continue.” It does not show the action or affected account. Is this meaningful oversight?
A person cannot make an informed choice without seeing what will happen and what information or people are affected.
Question 8. An agent sent an incorrect time to a family group. What should the parent do first?
Stop further effects and correct the information for affected people; then inspect how the error occurred and adjust permissions or workflow.
Question 9. A one-time itinerary task is complete, and the agent still has access to location history. What is a prudent next step?
Permissions should last only as long as needed. Remove access after the task when ongoing access has no clear purpose.
Question 10. A family asks an agent to make a decision about whether a child should attend an event during a severe weather warning. The system has conflicting reports. What should happen?
Safety-relevant uncertainty requires a human to consult official, current sources and make the decision; the agent should pause rather than choose.
